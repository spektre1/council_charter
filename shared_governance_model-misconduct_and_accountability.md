# Misconduct & Accountability Policy

## Draft 0.1a

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how misconduct by council members, stewards, auditors, mediators, safety workers, or participating representatives is handled.

The goal is not punishment for its own sake. The goal is to protect legitimacy, repair process failures, prevent abuse of power, and preserve community trust.

Accountability should be proportional, documented, appealable, and protected from factional misuse.

---

# 2. Core Principles

**Proportionality prevents overreach.**
Consequences should fit the seriousness of the conduct, the intent, the harm caused, the risk created, and the person’s pattern of behavior.

**Due process prevents mob rule.**
No serious consequence should be imposed based only on rumor, factional pressure, or public outrage.

**Accountability prevents prestige laundering.**
Popularity, founder status, trauma history, social capital, or activist language should not shield someone from review.

**Appeal prevents irreversible mistakes.**
Serious findings and consequences should have a defined appeal path.

**Anti-retaliation prevents fear.**
People must be able to report misconduct, disclose conflicts, cooperate with review, or challenge process without retaliation.

---

# 3. Categories of Misconduct

Misconduct may include:

## 3.1 Conflict-of-Interest Misconduct

* failing to disclose a relevant conflict of interest
* hiding a romantic, sexual, financial, factional, or close personal relationship relevant to a matter
* participating in a vote or decision despite a known conflict
* influencing others behind the scenes after recusal
* using private relationships to steer moderation, sanctions, endorsements, or coalition decisions

## 3.2 Confidentiality and Records Misconduct

* leaking sealed or restricted information
* exposing victim, witness, or vulnerable-person information
* sharing screenshots, Discord IDs, medical details, trauma disclosures, or doxxable context without authorization
* accessing records without a legitimate role
* altering, deleting, or withholding required records
* using sealed information for gossip, leverage, retaliation, or reputation warfare

## 3.3 Abuse of Authority

* using council, moderation, audit, or safety powers for personal gain
* retaliatory bans or quiet blacklisting
* using safety language to pursue factional goals
* laundering rumors into official action
* pressuring people into mediation, disclosure, silence, forgiveness, or public statements
* claiming authority the person or body does not have

## 3.4 Process Misconduct

* obstructing an investigation or appeal
* refusing required recusal
* ignoring review clocks or sunset clauses
* making private governance decisions without logging them
* excluding affected parties from required process
* failing to preserve evidence
* knowingly misrepresenting council decisions

## 3.5 Safety and Security Misconduct

* reckless handling of compromised accounts, phishing, or doxxing risks
* exposing evidence storage or private logs
* unsafe Discord/VRC permission practices after warning
* failure to contain known account compromise
* mishandling reports involving minors or vulnerable people

## 3.6 Conduct Unbecoming of a Steward

* harassment
* coercion
* intimidation
* sexual misconduct
* discriminatory conduct
* repeated bad-faith communication
* chronic failure to perform duties
* repeated avoidable escalation
* conduct that materially undermines trust in the person’s role

# 4. Severity Levels

## Level 1: Correctable Process Failure

Examples:

* late disclosure of a minor conflict
* accidental procedural omission
* missed documentation with no apparent harm
* misunderstanding of record requirements
* low-risk communication error

Possible responses:

* correction of the record
* reminder or coaching
* required training
* informal warning
* temporary recusal from the specific matter

## Level 2: Negligent Misconduct

Examples:

* failure to disclose a relevant close friendship
* participating in a matter where neutrality was reasonably impaired
* careless handling of restricted information
* repeated missed documentation
* ignoring a review deadline
* creating avoidable confusion in an active matter

Possible responses:

* formal warning
* mandatory recusal
* decision review
* temporary loss of role privileges
* required retraining
* public notation of recusal without private details
* sealed ethics note

## Level 3: Serious or Reckless Misconduct

Examples:

* knowingly failing to disclose a significant conflict
* voting or steering a decision despite a known conflict
* leaking restricted information
* retaliatory moderation
* misuse of authority
* obstruction of review
* pressuring parties in mediation
* repeated negligence after warning

Possible responses:

* suspension from council/guild duties
* removal from the matter
* voiding or reopening affected decisions
* temporary ineligibility for leadership roles
* public non-sensitive summary
* sealed ethics finding
* required third-party review before return

## Level 4: Corrupt, Predatory, or Dangerous Misconduct

Examples:

* intentional concealment of a conflict to influence an outcome
* doxxing or enabling doxxing
* leaking sealed victim/witness information
* evidence tampering
* coordinated retaliation
* weaponized false reports
* sexual coercion or predatory conduct
* using the council as a factional enforcement tool
* repeated serious misconduct after prior consequences

Possible responses:

* removal from office
* removal from council/guild eligibility
* referral to platform Trust & Safety where appropriate
* public non-sensitive statement
* sealed evidence preservation
* review of all affected prior decisions
* temporary or permanent disqualification from stewardship roles

# 5. Failure to Disclose a Conflict of Interest

Failure to disclose a conflict should be evaluated based on:

* whether the person reasonably should have known it was relevant
* whether they participated in the affected decision
* whether they influenced others privately
* whether they corrected the omission voluntarily
* whether harm or unfair advantage resulted
* whether there is a pattern of similar nondisclosure
* whether the nondisclosure appears accidental, negligent, reckless, or intentional

## Default Response

When a possible undisclosed conflict is discovered:

1. The person is temporarily recused from the matter.
2. The ethics/ombuds function conducts a preliminary review.
3. The conflict is logged in restricted or sealed form.
4. Any affected decision is reviewed for taint.
5. A proportional consequence is applied if needed.
6. A non-sensitive public correction is issued if the public record was affected.

## Example Public Language

"Council Member A has disclosed or been found to have a conflict of interest and is recused from this matter. The affected process is under ethics review. Private details will remain sealed."

# 6. Process for Handling Misconduct

## Step 1: Intake

A complaint or concern may be submitted to the ethics/ombuds function.

Reports should include:

* what happened
* who was involved
* what role the person held
* what decision or process was affected
* what evidence exists
* whether urgent harm is ongoing

## Step 2: Preliminary Review

The ethics/ombuds function determines:

* whether the issue falls within scope
* whether emergency protective action is needed
* whether conflicts require reassignment
* whether the matter should be public, restricted, or sealed
* whether the report appears good-faith, mistaken, malicious, or incomplete

## Step 3: Temporary Measures

Temporary measures may include:

* recusal
* temporary suspension of role access
* preservation of records
* restricted access to sealed material
* temporary pause on affected decisions
* referral to another role or body

Temporary measures are not final findings and must have review deadlines.

## Step 4: Review / Investigation

The reviewer gathers relevant information, distinguishes claims from evidence, and gives the accused person a meaningful chance to respond unless immediate safety prevents it.

## Step 5: Finding

The finding should state:

* what was alleged
* what was established
* what evidence was relied on
* what policy was violated, if any
* what consequence is imposed
* what review or appeal path exists
* what can be public and what must remain sealed

## Step 6: Outcome

Outcomes should be recorded in public, restricted, or sealed form depending on sensitivity.

Public summaries should be non-sensitive and should avoid exposing victims, witnesses, private relationships, or doxxable information.

## Step 7: Appeal

Serious findings should be appealable to a body or reviewer not involved in the original decision.

Appeals may be based on:

* new evidence
* procedural error
* disproportionate consequence
* conflict of interest
* bias or retaliation
* misapplication of policy

---

# 7. Remedies and Consequences

Possible remedies include:

* correction of the public record
* private warning
* formal warning
* training or mentorship
* required apology or repair process where appropriate
* recusal
* loss of access to restricted/sealed records
* temporary suspension from duties
* removal from a specific matter
* removal from office
* temporary ineligibility for council/guild roles
* permanent disqualification from stewardship roles in severe cases
* reopening or voiding affected decisions
* referral to VRChat Trust & Safety or Discord Trust & Safety where appropriate

Consequences should not be more public than necessary to protect process, safety, and legitimacy.

---

# 8. Anti-Abuse Protections

This policy must not be used for:

* factional retaliation
* punishing criticism
* suppressing whistleblowers
* laundering rumors
* forcing public disclosure of private relationships
* compelling victims to participate in processes they do not consent to
* protecting status rather than safety
* manufacturing pretexts to remove political opponents

Reports made in bad faith may themselves constitute misconduct.

Good-faith reports that cannot be substantiated should not be punished.

---

# 9. Public Summary Standard

When public notice is required, the council should disclose only what is necessary.

Good public summary:

"Council Member A has been recused due to a disclosed conflict of interest. The affected decision will be reviewed by the ethics function."

Bad public summary:

"Council Member A secretly dated Group B’s leader and hid it during a harassment case."

The first protects process.

The second creates gossip, humiliation, and collateral harm.

---

# 10. Short Version

Misconduct policy exists because trust requires consequences.

But consequences must be proportional, documented, appealable, and protected from factional misuse.

The standard is:

**Mistakes get correction.**
**Negligence gets review and restriction.**
**Recklessness gets suspension.**
**Corruption gets removal.**

The safety system needs its own safety system.
