# VRC Social Good Governance Framework

## Draft 0.1a: For Discussion

This is a rough synthesis of two related ideas:

1. A framework for identifying and supporting VRChat groups that promote social cohesion, good faith, belonging, trust, safety, comfort, and sustainable community growth.

2. A governance structure that prevents any council, agency, guild, or curation body from becoming opaque, captured, clique-driven, punitive, or socially radioactive.

The goal is not to create a corporate hierarchy, a universal moral court, a surveillance system, or a blacklist machine.

The goal is to create lightweight shared governance for groups that voluntarily recognize common standards and want to improve the health of the wider ecosystem.

---

# 1. Core Thesis

VRChat groups can create real social value.

People often stay in VRChat because they find durable social bonds, affinity groups, safety, belonging, identity, comfort, and mutual support. Some groups reproduce these conditions better than others. These groups can be understood as **social-good groups**.

A social-good group is not merely a large group, popular group, or ToS-compliant group. It is a group that demonstrates good faith, principled purpose, effective moderation, member recognition, leadership reproduction, and staying power.

The project, then, is not simply:

"Which groups should be featured?"

It is:

"How do we identify, support, reproduce, and protect the conditions that allow social-good groups to exist?"

---

# 2. The Governance Problem

Any curation or council process introduces power.

That power can help coordinate safety, education, shared standards, group mentorship, event discovery, and inter-group trust.

But that same power can also be captured, abused, hidden, factionalized, or converted into social capital.

So the framework needs to solve two problems at once:

1. How do we identify and support groups that promote the social good?
2. How do we prevent the body doing that work from becoming the problem?

This means the governance layer is not optional. It is the safety system for the safety system.

---

# 3. Proposed Founding-Document Bundle

Rather than one monolithic constitution, this should probably become a small founding-doc package:

1. **Charter / Constitution**
   Mission, scope, legitimacy, rights, limits, and institutional structure.

2. **Curation Standard**
   What qualifies as a social-good group, what evidence is considered, and what anti-patterns disqualify or delay recognition.

3. **Council Bylaws**
   Composition, seats, terms, voting, meetings, removal, recusal, emergency authority, and amendment process.

4. **Records & Observability Policy**
   Public record, restricted record, sealed record, metadata logging, review windows, audit rights, and public summaries.

5. **Conflict of Interest Policy**
   Relationships, factional ties, financial interests, close friendships, exes, recusal, and sealed disclosure.

6. **Incident / Mediation / Appeals Policy**
   Escalation standards, evidence handling, mediation, temporary measures, appeals, and review clocks.

7. **Security Hygiene Baseline**
   Discord/VRC compromise, phishing, evidence handling, permissions, account safety, and admin controls.

8. **Mentorship / Growth Path**
   How groups not ready for recognition can improve without being publicly shamed or permanently excluded.

# 4. Proposed Institutions

## 4.1 The Council

The Council provides public legitimacy, shared standards, curation guidance, inter-group coordination, and long-term stewardship.

The Council should not directly own or control participating groups.

Its legitimacy comes from voluntary recognition, transparent process, defined scope, accountable procedure, and community trust.

The Council’s role is to:

* maintain shared standards
* recognize social-good groups
* coordinate inter-group safety where appropriate
* support curation and mentorship
* maintain public records and summaries
* charter working groups or guilds
* provide appeal and review pathways
* prevent factional warfare from becoming ecosystem collapse

## 4.2 The Safety / Stewardship Guild

The Safety or Stewardship Guild provides operational support.

Its work may include:

* group-leader education
* moderation workshops
* anti-harassment readiness
* phishing and compromise response
* Discord/VRC security hygiene
* safety templates
* incident-response playbooks
* optional peer review
* group-readiness assessments
* mediation support
* burnout prevention for stewards

This should be framed as support infrastructure, not policing.

## 4.3 Ethics / Ombuds / Audit Function

The ethics, ombuds, or audit function exists to prevent capture and abuse of process.

Its role may include:

* reviewing sealed records when appropriate
* managing conflict-of-interest disclosures
* ensuring recusals happen
* investigating process abuse
* reviewing appeals
* auditing the Council and Guild
* ensuring public summaries are produced
* ensuring emergency authority does not become permanent authority

This function should be independent from the people who investigate, decide, enforce, or benefit from a matter.

# 5. Constitutional Principles

## 5.1 Observability, Not Surveillance

The public must be able to observe the machinery of governance without receiving private harm details.

Observability means the public can see:

* agendas
* votes
* policy decisions
* public summaries
* recusals
* review dates
* sealed-record metadata
* committee activity
* anonymized statistics
* audit outcomes
* appeal pathways
* whether the Council is following its own rules

Observability does **not** mean exposing:

* victim identities
* witness identities
* screenshots
* Discord IDs
* medical details
* trauma disclosures
* private relationship details
* doxxable context
* active investigation evidence
* sensitive platform Trust & Safety material

The public should know the system is not operating as a black box. Sensitive details should remain protected.

**Observability prevents secret power.**

## 5.2 Private Relationships Are Private. Conflicts of Interest Are Not.

The Council does not police dating, sex, friendships, exes, private intimacy, or personal relationships.

However, when romantic, sexual, intimate, financial, factional, or close personal relationships could reasonably affect governance, moderation, sanctions, endorsements, investigations, event access, coalition votes, resource-sharing, or member safety, they become conflicts of interest.

The private relationship remains private.

The conflict of interest must be disclosed, managed, and documented through the proper ethics/ombuds function.

Publicly, this may simply appear as:

"Leader A has disclosed a conflict of interest and is recused from this matter."

No outing. No gossip. No humiliation. No relationship policing.

Just governance hygiene.

**Private relationships are private. Conflicts of interest are not.**

## 5.3 Confidentiality Protects People. Secrecy Protects Power.

Sensitive information should be protected when disclosure would cause harm.

But sealed processes must not become invisible power.

A sealed matter should have metadata:

* general category
* participating groups or roles
* date opened
* review deadline
* oversight role notified
* current status
* final non-sensitive outcome summary when possible

The contents may remain sealed, but the existence of the sealed matter should not disappear.

**Confidentiality protects people. Secrecy protects power.**

## 5.4 Private Conversation Is Allowed. Private Governance Decisions Are Not Valid Until Logged.

Leaders may speak privately as humans.

The Council should not try to forbid private human conversation. That would be authoritarian, creepy, and impossible to enforce.

But if a private conversation produces an official action, recommendation, sanction, endorsement, coalition position, access decision, safety decision, or moderation outcome, that outcome must enter the Council record in public, restricted, or sealed form.

The model is:

**Private human conversation: allowed.**
**Private governance decision: not valid until logged.**
**Sensitive information: may be sealed, but not invisible.**

# 6. Record Layers

## 6.1 Public Record

The public record may include:

* agendas
* votes
* policy decisions
* non-sensitive summaries
* financials, if any
* committee activity
* public safety reports
* anonymized incident statistics
* recusals
* existence of sealed proceedings
* review dates
* final non-sensitive outcome summaries

## 6.2 Restricted Record

The restricted record contains sensitive but Council-relevant material available only to designated stewards, ombuds, ethics reviewers, elected auditors, or other approved roles.

This may include:

* sensitive incident context
* limited evidence summaries
* inter-group safety coordination
* moderation coordination
* internal review notes
* confidential but non-sealed process materials

## 6.3 Sealed Record

The sealed record is for highly sensitive material, including:

* doxxing risks
* abuse reports
* legal threats
* compromised accounts
* minors or vulnerable people
* active investigations
* platform Trust & Safety cooperation
* private relationship disclosures
* retaliation risks
* evidence that could cause direct harm if exposed

The contents remain sealed, but the existence, category, opening date, review date, and oversight role are logged.

The norm should be:

**Minutes by default. Summaries when sensitive. Sealed annexes when necessary.**

---

# 7. Private / Off-Record Leadership Communication

Cross-group leadership communication concerning official coalition matters should occur in Council-recognized channels by default.

Private/off-record communication may be used when confidentiality is justified by:

* protecting a victim, witness, or vulnerable person
* active harassment, doxxing, or stalking
* compromised Discord/VRC accounts or phishing response
* legal or platform Trust & Safety sensitivity
* mental health crisis or abuse disclosure
* avoiding retaliation while facts are verified
* mediation before public escalation
* pre-publication review of allegations
* security vulnerability or operational security issue
* good-faith conflict de-escalation where public discussion would inflame harm

Any resulting action, recommendation, sanction, endorsement, or policy position must be entered into the Council record, either publicly or as a sealed/restricted record subject to later audit.

## Non-Qualifying Reasons for Secrecy

Private/off-record channels should not be used for:

* avoiding accountability
* hiding factional coordination
* arranging retaliation
* laundering rumors into official action
* evading conflict-of-interest rules
* making coalition decisions without records
* excluding affected parties from due process
* protecting someone’s status rather than someone’s safety

---

# 8. Conflict of Interest Policy

A conflict of interest exists when a leader, moderator, safety worker, Council member, auditor, steward, or participating representative has a private relationship or personal stake that could reasonably affect their neutrality in an official matter.

Potential conflicts include:

* current romantic, sexual, or intimate relationships
* recent former romantic, sexual, or intimate relationships
* close friendships or chosen-family bonds
* severe interpersonal conflict or unresolved breakup fallout
* financial relationships
* business partnerships
* shared ownership of servers, groups, worlds, assets, or platforms
* factional alliances
* prior allegations of harm involving the parties
* personal vendettas or reputation disputes
* dependency relationships
* mentor/protégé relationships where power is uneven

Disclosure does not mean punishment.

Disclosure means the system can decide whether recusal, third-party review, extra documentation, or sealed oversight is needed.

Disclosure obligations may apply to recent former relationships, especially within the past 6–12 months, or where the relationship ended with unresolved conflict, allegations of harm, or reasonable concern that neutrality could be affected.

## Possible Responses to a Conflict

A disclosed conflict may require:

* recusal from votes
* recusal from disciplinary decisions
* a neutral third-party mediator
* sealed review by ombuds/ethics
* transfer of incident handling to uninvolved stewards
* extra documentation for decisions affecting either group
* public notation that a conflict exists, without private details

# 9. Curation Standard for Social-Good Groups

A social-good group is a group that demonstrates the ability and willingness to promote social cohesion, good faith, belonging, trust, safety, comfort, and sustainable community growth.

Relevant indicators may include:

## 9.1 Principled Purpose

The group has a clear core identity, purpose, focus, meaning, or character.

Single-purpose groups may be more cohesive, while complex-identity groups may need stronger governance to prevent schism.

## 9.2 Trust and Consistency

The group demonstrates:

* consensual interaction norms
* good-faith member conduct
* effective moderation
* willingness to engage with conflict
* consistent rule application
* safer/brave-space practices where appropriate

## 9.3 Belonging and Safety

The group creates conditions where members feel:

* recognized
* heard
* safer than they would be alone
* able to grow
* able to participate without fear of arbitrary exclusion

## 9.4 Maturity and Staying Power

The group demonstrates:

* history
* stability
* leadership reproduction
* knowledge sharing
* manageable churn
* institutional memory
* ability to scale with new users
* ability to survive leadership changes

## 9.5 Growth Path

Groups not ready for recognition should not simply be discarded.

They should be offered a path to improvement through mentorship, workshops, templates, or peer review.

# 10. Anti-Patterns

Groups may be delayed, denied recognition, or referred to mentorship if they show strong anti-patterns such as:

* cults of personality
* laissez-faire structure where no one is accountable
* leader worship
* social-capital chasing
* schism and radicalization dynamics
* "follow ToS" as the only real rule
* eristic communication, or arguing only to win
* opaque moderation
* retaliation against critics
* rumor laundering
* unsafe conflict handling
* inability to reproduce leadership
* single points of failure
* unmanaged leader burnout
* factional capture
* predatory or coercive leadership dynamics

Platform ToS is necessary but insufficient. Bad-faith groups can mimic ToS compliance while undermining trust and safety.

# 12. Due Process and Evidence

No person or group should be sanctioned, excluded, publicly condemned, or structurally harmed based on rumor, factional pressure, popularity contests, or vibes alone.

The Council must distinguish between:

* claims
* concerns
* testimony
* corroboration
* patterns
* material evidence
* direct risk
* immediate danger

Not every concern needs to become a public case.

Not every rumor should become an incident.

Not every accusation should become a sanction.

Escalation requires a clear standard:

* what is known
* how it is known
* who is at risk
* how urgent the risk is
* what action is proportionate
* what review or appeal path exists

Emergency action may happen first when necessary, but review must follow.

---

# 13. Appeals and Review

Every serious action should have a path for appeal, correction, or review.

Appeal does not mean endless relitigation.

It means the system admits that mistakes happen, evidence changes, bias exists, and process can fail.

Temporary measures need review dates.

Sanctions need expiration or renewal standards.

Sealed records need audit pathways.

Possible review clocks:

* 14 days for urgent temporary measures
* 30 days for sealed incident status review
* 60 days for extended restrictions or unresolved mediation
* 90 days for pilot-program evaluation
* annual review for policies, offices, and standards

---

# 14. Separation of Powers

The same people should not control every stage of a process.

Whenever possible, separate:

* intake
* investigation
* mediation
* decision-making
* enforcement
* appeal
* audit

No clique should be able to accuse, investigate, judge, punish, audit itself, and deny appeal.

---

# 15. Anti-Retaliation

Members, witnesses, victims, moderators, leaders, auditors, and participating groups must be protected from retaliation when they:

* report harm
* disclose conflicts
* criticize governance
* participate in appeals
* refuse improper pressure
* cooperate with audit
* request mediation
* challenge process

Retaliation may include:

* bans
* quiet blacklisting
* rumor campaigns
* reputational sabotage
* event denial
* social exclusion
* doxxing threats
* harassment
* abuse of moderation powers
* weaponized safety claims

A safety system that cannot protect people from retaliation will become performative.

---

# 16. Security Hygiene Is Community Safety

Cybersecurity is not separate from queer safety online.

Discord compromise, phishing, leaked mod logs, bad permissions, exposed evidence folders, weak admin accounts, doxxing, impersonation, and account takeovers can all become real community harm.

Basic security hygiene should be treated as part of the safety mandate.

The Guild may provide guidance on:

* Discord permissions
* role separation
* phishing response
* account compromise response
* evidence preservation
* secure evidence storage
* admin account hygiene
* incident logging
* compromise reporting
* containment procedures

---

# 17. Scope and Limits

The Council should not attempt to govern all queer life in VRChat.

It should not become a universal moral court.

It should not turn every interpersonal conflict into a coalition matter.

Its authority should apply only to:

* participating groups
* shared safety standards
* cross-group incidents
* coalition events
* moderation coordination
* public endorsements
* curation or recognition processes
* agreed-upon governance matters

Participation should be voluntary, legible, and reversible under defined conditions.

---

# 18. Emergency Powers

Emergency action may be necessary when there is immediate risk of harm.

However, emergency powers must be narrow, temporary, documented, and reviewable.

Emergency action should require:

* stated reason
* limited duration
* responsible role
* review deadline
* later summary
* appeal or audit path where appropriate

Emergency powers must not become normal governance.

**Sunset clauses prevent zombie authority.**

---

# 19. Steward Care and Burnout Prevention

Safety work burns people out.

Moderators, mediators, auditors, and leaders need:

* backup
* boundaries
* decompression
* role limits
* training
* rotation
* mutual support
* the ability to step away without collapsing the system

A governance model that consumes its caretakers will eventually be run by the people least affected by burnout: opportunists, obsessives, and power-seekers.

---

# 20. Non-Affiliation and Platform Relationship

This framework should be clear about whether it is:

* independent from VRChat
* seeking platform recognition
* advising platform curation
* operating as a community coalition
* piloting a model for possible platform partnership

Until formally recognized, it should not imply that it speaks for VRChat, acts as VRChat Trust & Safety, or has platform enforcement power.

It may coordinate with platform Trust & Safety when appropriate, but it should not replace platform reporting channels, legal processes, emergency services, or professional crisis support.

# 21. Missing Implementation Questions

Before this becomes operational, the following questions need answers:

## Membership and Seats

* Who gets a Council seat?
* Are seats elected, appointed, rotating, or mixed?
* Do large groups get more representation?
* Are there seats for unaffiliated community members?
* Are there seats for auditors, mediators, or technical/security people?

## Voting

* What decisions require simple majority?
* What decisions require supermajority?
* What decisions require consensus?
* What decisions can be made by working groups?
* What decisions require public comment first?

## Removal and Recall

* How can a Council member be removed?
* Who can trigger review?
* What evidence is required?
* Is there an appeal?
* What happens if a group’s representative is removed?

## Amendments

* How are founding documents changed?
* Is there public notice?
* Is there a comment period?
* What threshold is required?

## Data Retention

* How long are public, restricted, and sealed records retained?
* Who can access sealed records?
* Are access logs kept?
* When are records destroyed?
* How is sensitive evidence stored?

## Pilot Metrics

* How do we know the pilot worked?
* What would failure look like?
* What would success look like?
* What feedback is collected from groups and members?

# 22. Suggested Pilot

Start small.

Do not begin with a grand claim of authority.

Suggested first pilot:

1. Select 3–5 willing groups.
2. Have each opt into a non-punitive readiness review.
3. Review against social-good criteria.
4. Offer private recommendations.
5. Provide security/moderation templates.
6. Test the public/restricted/sealed records model.
7. Test COI disclosure and recusal procedures.
8. Produce an anonymized lessons-learned report.
9. Revise the framework before expanding.

The first version should be boring, procedural, and obviously benevolent.

The more it looks like infrastructure, the safer it is.

The more it looks like an intelligence apparatus, the more everyone’s trauma alarms go off.
